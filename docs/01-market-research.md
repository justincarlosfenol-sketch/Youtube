### 1.2 Money per view

- **Shorts ad RPM is ~$0.03–$0.10 per 1,000 views (est.); long-form is ~$2–$30.** The only large measured sample ([AIR, 300 channels, May 2025–May 2026](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/youtube-monetization-statistics-2026)) reports a **median long-form RPM of $2.30** across all niches. → Shorts are a *discovery* tool, not an income stream. ([AIR Shorts vs long-form](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/youtube-shorts-rpm-vs-long-form-how-much-do-shorts-earn-in-2026))
- **Niche RPMs conflict.** AIR's measured data ranks Education & Science first (median RPM **$10.22**) and puts "Business & Finance" at **$2.01**. Vendor estimates put US personal finance at **$8–$25+** ([ytcalculators](https://ytcalculators.com/blog/youtube-rpm-by-niche/), [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-niches-guide/), [fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/personal-finance-content-creator-guide-usa)). How we reconcile them: AIR's "finance" bucket is dominated by global, crypto and trading channels with low-value audiences. RPM is driven by **audience geography × purchase intent**, not by the label. A US-centred, decision-oriented personal finance channel (mortgages, retirement accounts, insurance, credit) sits at the high end. **Our financial model uses $6 / $10 / $15 RPM (conservative / moderate / optimistic), well below vendor claims.**
- **Sponsorship CPMs for finance creators are the highest of any category:** $40–$80 ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-sponsorship-rates/)) up to $50–$200 ([Creators Agency](https://creatorsagency.co/blog/youtube-sponsorship-rates-personal-finance-creators-2026)), priced on average views (est., vendor sources).
- **Finance affiliate payouts are among the largest on the internet:** roughly $14–$455+ per conversion depending on product (brokerages, HYSA, budgeting apps, insurance, credit). Programs run mostly via Impact / FlexOffers and often require a website with original content. ([Fintel Connect](https://www.fintelconnect.com/?p=1731707), [Acorns via Affililist](https://www.affililist.com/blog/acorns-affiliate-program), [LinkClicky](https://linkclicky.com/affiliate-programs/banking/))

### 1.3 What is growing

- Fastest-growing channels by raw subscribers are entertainment and gaming driven by Shorts ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/top/fastest-growing-youtube-channels/), [Branzly](https://www.branzly.com/youtube-insights/fastest-growing-youtube-channels-2026)). **24% of large fast-growing channels upload 60+ times a month** ([CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/creator-news/fastest-growing-youtube-channels-content-farms/)). That is the content-farm playbook YouTube is now actively demonetizing. Subscriber velocity in low-RPM entertainment is a vanity metric for our purposes.
- Faceless AI-voiced drama/revenge storytelling is booming ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-drama-trends-2026/)). It has a low RPM, maximum policy exposure and no moat. **Rejected.**

### Why the low scores are low
- **#3 AI tools / #7 markets news:** high RPM but perishable. A tutorial is stale in months, and market commentary is a daily treadmill with heavy competition and compliance exposure.
- **#9 tech reviews:** needs physical products, on-camera presence and launch-day speed. We can't win on those.
- **#10 health:** strict YMYL scrutiny and medical-misinformation policy risk; a channel-killing error is expensive.
- **#12 faceless AI storytelling:** exactly the inauthentic-content pattern YouTube is demonetizing.
- **#2 company breakdowns:** strong but crowded (ColdFusion, Modern MBA, Company Man, Logically Answered, Bloomberg Originals). **We fold it in as a secondary series** ("The money behind…") because it shares the same data-viz pipeline.
- **#11 career/salary data:** a good adjacent vertical. **Planned as a series inside the channel** (career decisions are money decisions).

## 3. The winner: **Breakeven**, data-simulated personal finance decisions

**One-line positioning:** *Every video takes one expensive real-life money decision, builds a transparent model with real data, and finds the breakeven point: the exact moment one choice beats the other.*

### Why it wins
1. **Highest-value audience on the platform.** People deciding whether to buy a house, refinance, max a 401(k), lease a car or pay off student loans are, by definition, about to move tens of thousands of dollars. That's why finance sponsors pay the highest CPMs and affiliates pay $50–$400 per conversion.
2. **Demand is permanent and search-driven.** "Rent vs buy", "Roth vs traditional", "pay off mortgage or invest", "is a car lease worth it" are asked every year by a new cohort. Search traffic compounds: a good video keeps earning for years and gets re-ranked on browse when rates or markets change. The topic set is also *re-runnable*: "Rent vs Buy 2027" with new data is a new, legitimately original video.
3. **A real quality gap.** Incumbents fall into four camps: (a) **talking heads with B-roll** (Graham Stephan, Humphrey Yang, The Money Guy Show), (b) **evidence-based but dry lectures** (Ben Felix, The Plain Bagel), (c) **illustrated explainers** that explain concepts but rarely *compute* anything (The Wolf Explains, Nick Explains Finance, How Money Works-style channels), and (d) **calculator websites** (Zillow, NerdWallet, NYT rent-vs-buy) with no storytelling. **Nobody consistently does cinematic, animated *quantitative* storytelling for personal decisions: "the calculator, as a documentary."** That is our lane.
4. **Our unfair advantage is engineering.** Our pipeline renders original, animated, data-driven visuals from code: every chart is computed from the model, every number traceable to a source. That makes (a) quality consistently high, (b) visuals 100% owned (no stock or copyright risk), (c) the content *provably original* (the strongest possible defence against inauthentic-content enforcement), and (d) production fast without being templated, because each video's *model* is unique.
5. **Monetization is unusually diversified:** AdSense, finance sponsors, affiliates (HYSA, brokerages, budgeting, insurance, mortgage marketplaces), **digital products** (the spreadsheet model behind every video), a **free calculator website** (SEO + email capture + affiliate placement), newsletter sponsorships, memberships, and licensing animated explainers to fintechs, credit unions and financial educators.
6. **Durable for years.** Interest rates, housing, retirement accounts and taxes will be debated for decades. The channel gets *more* valuable as the library of models grows.

### Key risks and mitigations
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Finance is YMYL; a wrong number damages trust | Every claim cites a primary source. Every model is published (spreadsheet/code). A human signs off on numbers before publishing. Corrections are pinned. Educational framing, never individualized advice. |
| Inauthentic/AI-slop classification | Human narrator (recommended: **you**, or a hired voice actor), original models in every video, no templates reused verbatim, slower cadence (1 flagship/week). Kokoro TTS is used only for drafts, or disclosed if published. |
| RPM may land at the low end ($2–5) | The model doesn't depend on AdSense: affiliate + product revenue per 1,000 views can exceed ad RPM in this niche. |
| 8,000-hour YPP bar | Long-form 9–14 min videos with high retention. Shorts drive discovery into long-form. Affiliate + products monetize before YPP. |
| Competitors copy the format | The moat is the accumulated model library, data pipeline, brand trust and email list, not the look. |

## 4. Competitor analysis

| Channel | Format | What works | Weakness we exploit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Graham Stephan** | Talking head, real-estate heavy, very frequent | Personality, timeliness, strong packaging ("I was wrong about…") | Opinion-led. Numbers are asserted rather than modelled on screen. |
| **Humphrey Yang** | Talking head + on-screen math, huge Shorts presence | Simple visual math, "$X becomes $Y" curiosity | Visual polish is functional; few rigorous assumptions shown. |
| **Ben Felix (PWL)** | Evidence-based lectures | Credibility, academic rigour, loyal audience; popularized the "5% rule" for rent vs buy | Dry delivery, low visual storytelling, narrow audience. |
| **The Plain Bagel** | Analyst explainer, light visuals | Trustworthy, measured | ~2 videos/month; markets focus rather than household decisions. |
| **The Money Guy Show** | Two-host show | Frameworks ("financial order of operations"), community | Long podcast-style runtimes; little visual data. |
| **The Wolf Explains / Nick Explains Finance** | Animated explainers | Proves demand for animated personal finance | Concept explainers; little original modelling or data. |
| **Calculator sites** (Zillow, NerdWallet, NYT) | Interactive tools | Huge search demand, trusted | No narrative and no "why". Our videos *are* the narrative version, and our site gives the tool. |

**Packaging patterns that work in this niche** (observed across the above): a single big **number** in the thumbnail; a **face or object with tension** (house, car, paycheck); **"vs"** framing; **time-bound** hooks ("in 2026", "at 7%"); **counter-intuitive results** ("buying loses for 9 years"); and **personal stakes** ("you'll lose $X"). Hooks open with the most surprising *computed* number, not an introduction.

**Opening-hook pattern we'll use (the "Breakeven cold open"):** (1) a surprising, verified number in the first 5 seconds; (2) the tension, "that should mean X… it doesn't"; (3) the promise: "by the end you'll know your own breakeven year"; (4) straight into chapter 1 within 30–40 seconds. No intro animation longer than 2 seconds.
